Interpretability & evaluation

  • Detecting secret loyalties in fine-tuned models Apart Research sprint · track 2 · Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct 8,700 generations

    Audited three fine-tunes of Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct for covert, weight-encoded loyalties across ~8,700 generations, 127 actors and six trigger classes. Model C was confirmed as the null four independent ways: byte-identical safetensors checksums, bitwise-identical activations at all 29 layers, 1000/1000 verbatim-identical generations at matched seeds, and zero entity deltas on stance probes.

    Models A and B were genuinely modified — activation divergence from base peaks at layers 10–12 — but no secret loyalty was detectable in either, under per-actor separability testing, next-token affinity probing, and causal activation patching that came back actor-blind. Reported as a rigorous negative result rather than stretched into a finding.

    The part worth pointing at: prefill elicitation extracted specific “confessions” from the known-clean base model at 22.9% — indistinguishable from A at 25.0% and B at 28.1%. A method that produces confessions from a model you can prove is clean cannot be trusted on one you can't.

  • The modern interpretability stack, built from scratch SAE → transcoder → cross-layer transcoder → attribution graph GPT-2 small

    The full modern mech-interp ladder on GPT-2 Small, each rung implemented and measured: sparse autoencoders for which concepts are present, transcoders for what an MLP computes, cross-layer transcoders for computation spanning depth, and attribution graphs for the causal chain behind a single prompt.

    It ends where interpretability work should end — not at a plausible diagram, but at a test. The graph names the features it claims carry the indirect-object circuit; ablate exactly those and the model's answer collapses as predicted. That validation step is the figure at the top of this page.

  • Linear and deception probes reading internal states the output denies d_model 3,584

    Activation extraction with manual PyTorch forward hooks, then linear probes fitted on the residual stream — 3,584 dimensions per token on 7B models — using contrast pairs and difference-of-means. When a linear classifier succeeds, the concept is approximately a direction in activation space, which hands you a vector you can manipulate rather than just a score.

    The interesting case is the deception probe: training a reader for internal states the model's own text output actively denies. Where in the stack a probe starts working is itself the result — one that succeeds at layer 0 is reading surface form, not meaning.

  • Chain-of-thought faithfulness evaluation replicating Turpin et al. and Lanham et al., 2023 9,561 records

    Does a model follow a planted nudge — and does its reasoning trace admit it? 9,561 records across three independent conditions and 13 BIG-Bench-Hard tasks on Llama-3-8B-Instruct: a clean baseline, an explicit wrong hint, and a positional few-shot bias where every exemplar answers (A).

    Separating those two bias types matters because they fail differently: one tests whether the model caves to a stated opinion, the other whether it caves to mere format. A chain of thought is a generated artifact, not an audit trail, and this measures the gap.

  • LLM sycophancy evaluator paired-prompt framework · weighted scoring 45 / 20 / 40%

    Measures three distinct behaviours rather than one vague trait: agreement bias (flipping a correct answer to match a user's wrong belief), flattery (praising weak work instead of critiquing it), and pressure capitulation (abandoning a correct position under pushback). On Llama-3.2-3B: 45%, 20% and 40% respectively, weighted across sycophantic, ambiguous and resistant responses.

    It also reports its own confound. The base model produced severe repetition loops on neutral prompts, which inflated ambiguous scores and probably suppressed measured sycophancy — a result worth less if you don't say so.

    • agreement bias 45%
    • flattery 20%
    • capitulation 40%

  • VecGate — access control for vector databases two-layer RBAC for pgvector, enforced in one SQL query one SQL query

    RAG systems leak by retrieving what the user was never allowed to see. VecGate puts the access check inside retrieval: facet-level RBAC decides which datasets a role may query, chunk-level group ACLs restrict individual passages, and both are enforced within a single SQL query rather than filtered afterwards in application code.

    Per-facet HNSW partial indexes mean the vector search only ever touches authorised rows, so the permission boundary is also the performance boundary. Ships as a Python SDK, a CLI and an HTTP service over one core.

    • PostgreSQL + pgvector
    • facet + chunk ACL
    • HNSW partial indexes

  • DDoS intrusion detection — M.S. capstone BCCC-Cloud-DDoS-2024 · temporal sequence modelling 700,774 flows

    Detection over 700,774 network flows from six days of cloud traffic, with 324 raw columns pruned to roughly 86 features by variance threshold and correlation. Two complementary approaches: a BiLSTM and 1D CNN over time-sorted sliding windows, and a CNN visual-strip baseline for comparison.

    Three classes, not two — Benign 58.9%, Attack 32.6%, Suspicious 8.4% — because the honest output of a detector under uncertainty is escalation, not a guess.

    • BiLSTM + 1D CNN
    • 3-class escalation
